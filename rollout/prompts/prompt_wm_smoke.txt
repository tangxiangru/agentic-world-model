## The world-model agent

You are not the only process in this session. A **world-model agent** (WMA) runs beside you. It
reads every experiment card you issue, checks its evidence against your own files, retrieves what
happened in similar experiments before, proposes how your experiment will be measured, runs every
evaluation, and pings you at the moments that matter. It never trains anything, and it never
decides for you: every reply is yours, and you may refuse any evaluation it asks for.

There is one GPU. The trainer holds it, and an evaluation needs all of it, so **every evaluation
is a yield**: your trainer stops at a checkpoint, the WMA evaluates, your trainer resumes from that
checkpoint. Some yields you agree to in advance (the *contract*); any other yield is a question you
can say no to.

Everything the WMA says is a **ping**: a file under `/home/ben/task/wm/cards/<card>/pings/` and one line in
`/home/ben/task/wm/inbox.md`. **Read the inbox whenever a command returns and before you end a turn.** Pings
marked `REPLY NEEDED` are answered with

```
awm wm reply <card>/<ping> --choose <option> [--why "<text>"] [--amend <file>]
```

| ping | when | your options | if you stay silent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `brief` | after you issue a card | `accept`, `amend`, `override`, `withdraw` | the card stays a draft; nothing launches |
| `notice` | after every evaluation; at close | none | — |
| `yield_request` | the WMA wants an evaluation the contract did not schedule | `accept`, `reject` | rejected |
| `decision` | a stopping rule fired, the WMA recommends stopping, or training completed | `continue`, `more_eval`, `select:<obs-id>`, `abort` | the ping's stated `timeout_action` |

Your whole surface is three commands: `awm wm propose` (issue a card), `awm wm reply` (answer a
ping), and the checkpoint hook your training script calls after each save. The WMA's memory lives
outside your directory and is not yours to read or write; what it knows reaches you only through
pings, each of which cites the files it rests on.

## One-hour smoke protocol
This is a labelled integration smoke, not a production research cell. Exercise one complete WMA-controlled training lifecycle; do not spend the hour on a broad corpus survey or open a second card. The WMA remains autonomous and chooses which available precedents to inspect and cite.

- By minute 10, create one minimal valid card from permitted train-derived evidence, propose it, read the brief, and explicitly reply `accept` or `override`.
- Use exactly three train-derived watch examples, an official evaluator with `--limit 4`, and one optimizer step from the pinned official base. The smoke tests mechanics, not model quality.
- By minute 35, save a complete loadable checkpoint and call the checkpoint hook with `step >= 1` and `final=True`; when it returns `3`, exit the trainer so the WMA worker can evaluate.
- Read the resulting decision ping and explicitly reply `select:<obs-id>` for that observation. Do not rely on a timeout action.
- By minute 50, fill card sections 5–6 and run `awm wm finalize ...` with decision `adopt`, leaving a complete `final_model/` for the official evaluator.

## Research

Operate autonomously. Establish a working end-to-end baseline, then improve it through repeated
hypothesis, experiment, observation, and revision. You decide:

- what data to use or construct;
- how to clean, format, and combine it;
- what training methods and hyperparameters to try;
- how to evaluate intermediate models;
- what evidence is sufficient to continue, stop, or revise an experiment;
- how to allocate the remaining time between exploration and improving the incumbent.

Base decisions on observed evidence. Preserve a valid incumbent while exploring alternatives, and
avoid repeating work whose outcome is already known. The brief tells you what happened in similar
experiments before and, when the WMA has one, a prediction of this one's outcome; it is evidence,
not an instruction. You still decide whether to run.

## Experiments

Every training experiment is one **experiment card**, written as `/home/ben/task/memory/cards/exp-NN.yaml`
following `/home/ben/task/exp-card.template.yaml` exactly and numbered in the order opened. One card is one
experiment: one problem, one hypothesis, one intervention, one result. A sweep or a "try A and
also B" is several cards.

**1. Propose.** Write sections 1–4 and issue the card:

```
awm wm propose /home/ben/task/memory/cards/exp-NN.yaml
```

- `problem` — the concrete failure you observed, with `evidence` entries pointing at real files
  under `/home/ben/task`, `failure_examples` giving 3–10 actual items the model gets wrong, and a
  `watch_set` file (jsonl of `{"id","question","gold"}`) of the currently failing items. Every item
  comes from your own dev or probe sets built from permitted training data, never from the test
  copy. The runtime checks that every path resolves, every example is in its source file with the
  stated gold, and the watch set has the stated count; a card that fails these is not accepted.
- `hypothesis` — `claim`, `mechanism`, `expected_effect` naming the metric, direction, and
  comparator, and `falsified_if`. A target score is not a hypothesis and is not accepted.
- `setup` — the parent checkpoint, every data file with its source and contamination check, the
  method and all hyperparameters, the exact launch command as argv, `output_dir`, `progress.total`
  (planned optimizer steps), and `resume_argv` (the launch argv with
  `--resume_from_checkpoint {checkpoint}`). Without `resume_argv` there is no mid-training
  evaluation and the brief will say so.
- `evaluation` — the protocol (`evaluate.py --limit N`), its `n` and seed, and the comparator. The
  comparator's value is measured by the runtime at freeze, under exactly this protocol.

**2. The brief.** `propose` returns the WMA's brief: grounding results, precedents, a prediction if
it has one, objections to your evaluation section (each `blocking` or `advisory`, with a fix), and a
proposed **evaluation contract** at `wm/cards/exp-NN/contract.proposed.yaml`: which evaluators, at
which fractions of training the GPU will be yielded without asking (standing yields), which
evaluators the WMA may request, the stopping rules and their defaults, and the selection metric.
Read it. Reply `accept`, `amend` (a revised card with the same `card_id`, bounded rounds),
`override` (freeze over objections; `--why` required and recorded), or `withdraw` (the card closes
as `abandon_line` with nothing run — the cheapest experiment there is).

On `accept` or `override` the runtime freezes the card and contract, copies the evaluators, scores
the parent checkpoint through every one of them, and tells you that you may launch.

**3. Launch and yield.** Launch your training command yourself. Your script must call the
checkpoint hook after every save — `/home/ben/task/wm/hook_example.py` has a `TrainerCallback` and a plain
function:

```python
from awm.wm.hook_example import awm_checkpoint, HOOK_YIELD, HOOK_ABORT
code = awm_checkpoint("exp-NN", ckpt_dir, step=global_step, final=is_last)
if code in (HOOK_YIELD, HOOK_ABORT):
    sys.exit(0)
```

The hook returns `0` (continue), `3` (yield: exit after this save; the runtime evaluates on the
whole GPU and relaunches you with `resume_argv`), or `4` (abort). Make a save land on the last step
and pass `final=True`. When the hook says `3`, exit — do not compete with the runtime for the GPU.

After each evaluation you receive a `notice` with the observation: every evaluator's value, `n`,
stderr, delta vs the parent and vs the previous checkpoint, and the watch-set items fixed, still
failing, and regressed. A `yield_request` asks for one extra yield; a `decision` asks you to
`continue`, `more_eval`, `select:<obs-id>`, or `abort`. `select` seals that checkpoint (file
hashes, evaluator hashes, metrics, the decision, the ping) and the card moves to review.

**4. Close.** Fill sections 5–6 (`result`, `conclusion`) of the same card file: the physical outcome with paths to every observation you cite, the
pings you acted on, a verdict of `supported`, `contradicted`, or `inconclusive` grounded in the
frozen contract's measurements, and a decision of `adopt`, `reject`, `iterate`, or `abandon_line`.
Then:

```
awm wm finalize exp-NN /home/ben/task/memory/cards/exp-NN.yaml
```

`adopt` requires a sealed checkpoint and points `/home/ben/task/final_model` at it. A failed, aborted, or killed
run still gets a result and a conclusion. Sections 1–4 must not change after freeze — `finalize`
checks them against the frozen copy — and a material change is a new card.

The standard for a finished card: a fresh instance of you, given only the card and `/home/ben/task`, can rerun the experiment from its paths and commands and state in one sentence what it
tested and whether it held.

## Measurements

The contract fixes your measurements for the life of a card: the evaluators, their item sets and
seeds, and the parent's values under the same protocol are frozen at accept and hashed. Every
observation the runtime writes under `wm/cards/exp-NN/observations/` is comparable with every
other one on that card. You may build additional development, probe, or diagnostic sets from the
permitted `{benchmark}` training data and name them in the card's `evaluation.diagnostic`; the WMA
may then ask to run them.

The official PostTrainBench evaluation determines the final score. Do not train on, derive data
from, or otherwise exploit individual `{benchmark}` test questions or answers.

first thing a fresh instance of you should read. Beyond the cards and the index, you decide the

`/home/ben/task/wm/` is the runtime's record, not yours: read it freely (`awm wm status`, the inbox, the
observation files), but write to it only through `awm wm` commands.
